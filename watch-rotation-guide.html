Building a Watch Rotation

A rotation is simply the set of watches you actually wear. Built well, it covers every part of your life without a drawer of watches you never touch.

Start with your week

List what you really do: office, travel, sport, formal events, weekends. Most people need fewer watches than they think. Three well-chosen watches cover nearly every situation.

The classic three

  • A dress watch: thin, simple dial, leather strap. Slips under a cuff.
  • A tough watch: diver or field watch, 100 m or more, for sport, travel and weekends.
  • The one you love: a chronograph, a vintage piece or something with a story.

Avoid near-duplicates

Two black-dial steel divers will fight for the same days and one will sit idle. Vary at least two of: size, dial colour, strap material and formality.

Use straps to stretch it

A quick-release leather, rubber or fabric strap changes a watch’s character in seconds. Many people find straps more satisfying than another watch.

Wear them in turn

Mechanical watches like to run. Wearing each regularly keeps oils moving and stops one sitting forgotten. Our week planner spreads them out for you.

Know when to stop

If a watch has not been worn in six months, ask whether it belongs in the rotation. Passing it on can make room for one you will actually wear.

“The best rotation is the one where every watch gets worn.”
